Community rules

How we keep the bazaar healthy.

These guidelines exist so that Digital Bazaar stays useful, warm and safe – not to over-police conversation. If in doubt, lean towards generosity, clarity and respect.

This is a living document. We may refine it as the community grows and new situations appear.

Short version: Be kind. Share generously. Keep things constructive and relevant. Don’t spam or harass people. Moderators can remove anyone who repeatedly makes the bazaar worse.
  1. 01
    Be kind and respectful

    Treat everyone in the bazaar with basic human decency. We come from different countries, backgrounds and disciplines – assume good intent, ask questions, and avoid personal attacks.

    We’re here to support and uplift each other in our professional journeys, not to score points.

  2. 02
    Share knowledge and opportunities freely

    The bazaar only works if people bring real stories, lessons and opportunities. Share what you know: things you’ve tried, pitfalls you’ve hit, resources you’ve found useful, roles you’re hiring for.

    You don’t need to be “an expert” to contribute – honest field notes are often more valuable than polished theory.

  3. 03
    Keep discussions constructive and on-topic

    Stay broadly within the world of tech, product, business, research and the digital ecosystem. Side quests are fine, but the main lanes are for useful, work-adjacent conversations.

    Disagreeing is welcome; dragging people or starting flame wars is not. Critique ideas and systems, not people or whole groups.

  4. 04
    No spam, no hard selling

    Share your work naturally – as part of a discussion, a story, or a genuine “here’s something that might help”. Don’t blast the same link everywhere or turn the bazaar into a sales funnel.

    If you’re not sure whether something is appropriate, add context: why you’re sharing it, who it’s for, and what kind of feedback or response you’re looking for.

  5. 05
    Jobs & hiring etiquette

    Job posts and contract opportunities belong in the dedicated Jobs lane / channel, not sprayed across every group.

    • Keep posts clear: role, company, location/remote, type (full-time, part-time, contract), and how to apply.
    • Don’t tag or DM people at random with offers they didn’t ask for.
    • If you’re looking for work, one well-written message is better than daily bumps.
  6. 06
    Professional yet warm

    The tone is deliberately informal – like a good café table at a conference – but this is still a professional space.

    Jokes are welcome; punching down is not. Share wins and losses, but respect privacy and avoid sharing sensitive information that isn’t yours to share.

  7. 07
    Moderators’ discretion

    Moderators can remove messages or members that are clearly making the space worse – spam, harassment, persistent bad-faith arguing, or ignoring repeated feedback.

    When possible, we’ll nudge and explain first. In more serious cases, we may remove people without warning to protect the rest of the community.

  8. 08
    Don’t be an asshole

    Zero tolerance for racism, sexism, religious hate, personal harassment, doxxing, or any kind of targeted abuse.

    If you’re not sure whether something crosses the line, don’t post it. If someone flags something to you, listen instead of doubling down.

See something off? If a message or interaction makes you uncomfortable, reach out to a moderator privately. We’d rather hear about it early than let it fester.

If all of this sounds good to you, you’ll probably enjoy the bazaar.
